IMO Decarbonization Targets & CII Ratings

The International Maritime Organization (IMO) has implemented mandatory measures to reduce carbon emissions from international shipping. Central to this strategy is the Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII).

EEDI & EEXI

The Energy Efficiency Design Index (EEDI) applies to newbuilds, whereas the Energy Efficiency Existing Ship Index (EEXI) sets limits for existing fleet performance. Both reflect the technical design efficiency of vessels.

CII Operational Rating

CII tracks operational performance annually. Ships receive an annual rating (A to E, where A is superior). A ship rated D for three consecutive years or E for one year must submit a corrective action plan.

Understanding the CII Bands:
Rating Status Performance Consequences
A Major Superior Highly optimized technical efficiency, eco-speed configurations No action required. Eligible for port incentives.
B Minor Superior Above-average operational management No action required.
C Moderate Baseline acceptable performance No action required. Recommended monitoring.
D Minor Inferior Below-average fuel efficiency Corrective action required if rating persists for 3 years.
E Inferior High emissions, inefficient machinery/operation Immediate corrective action plan required in SEEMP.

Fast Facts

  • 🌍 Global Impact
    Maritime shipping transports ~90% of global trade and accounts for 2.9% of global greenhouse emissions.
  • 🎯 IMO Goal 2050
    The IMO target is to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions in international shipping by or around 2050.
  • ⚡ Speed Reduction
    Reducing vessel speed by 10% can lower fuel consumption and greenhouse gas emissions by up to 30%.
Did you know?

Bulk carriers typically maintain a lower CO₂ Index (g/nm) due to slower speeds and higher payloads compared to high-speed Ro-Ro cargo vessels.
